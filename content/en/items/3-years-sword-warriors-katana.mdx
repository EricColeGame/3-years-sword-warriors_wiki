Forging a high-grade blade demands intense precision. The steel undergoes repeated heating, hammering, and quenching processes that alter its crystalline structure. Below is a breakdown of the primary structural components found on a traditional hand-forged sword:

| Component | Japanese Term | Function & Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Blade | *Katana* / *Blade* | The curved, single-edged striking surface, typically crafted from high carbon steel. |
| Handguard | *Tsuba* | A metal or alloy guard protecting the wielder's hand from sliding onto the edge. |

## Training Disciplines and Maintenance Protocols

Owning a sharpened carbon-steel weapon carries significant responsibility. Maintaining a **3 years sword warriors katana** requires a strict regimen of cleaning, oiling, and inspecting. Sweat, moisture, and natural skin oils can quickly degrade high carbon steel, leading to pitting and rust if left unattended. 

To ensure longevity, practitioners should follow a standardized maintenance schedule:

| Maintenance Task | Recommended Frequency | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Wipe down blade with micro-fiber cloth | After every handling | Removes fingerprints, dust, and acidic skin oils. |
| Apply light mineral or choji oil | Monthly (or after heavy use) | Creates a protective barrier against moisture and oxidation. |
| Inspect *tsukamaki* (cord wrap) | Bi-annually | Checks for loose cotton or synthetic wrapping on the handle. |
| Examine *habaki* and *saya* fit | Annually | Ensures the blade seats securely without rattling or scratching. |

Beyond physical maintenance, martial artists spend countless hours refining their stances. The repetition builds core strength, improves hand-eye coordination, and deepens the practitioner's connection to the weapon.
